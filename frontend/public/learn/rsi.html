The RSI indicator

Intermediate · Trading

The idea

The Relative Strength Index (RSI) is a momentum indicator from 0 to 100 that compares recent gains with recent losses, usually over 14 periods.

How it works

  • Above 70 is often called "overbought" (rose fast); below 30 "oversold" (fell fast).
  • In strong trends RSI can stay above 70 or below 30 for a long time.
  • Divergence — price making a new high while RSI makes a lower high — can hint momentum is fading.

Worked example

A stock rises 9 days out of 10 and RSI climbs to 78. That does not mean it must fall; in a strong uptrend RSI often sits between 60 and 80 for weeks.

Common mistakes

  • Selling just because RSI is above 70 in a strong uptrend.
  • Using RSI alone without considering trend or news.

Key takeaways

  • RSI measures momentum on a 0–100 scale.
  • 70/30 are guides, not rules.
  • Look for divergences and the overall trend.
