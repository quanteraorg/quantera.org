Cash flow and free cash flow

Intermediate · Accounting

The idea

Cash flow tracks real money moving in and out of a business. Profit can be shaped by accounting choices; cash is much harder to fake.

How it works

  • Operating cash flow: cash generated by the core business.
  • Capital expenditure (capex): money spent on equipment, buildings, technology.
  • Free cash flow = operating cash flow − capex: cash left for dividends, buybacks, debt repayment or growth.

Worked example

A company reports $500M profit but only $150M of operating cash flow because customers have not paid yet. After $100M of capex, free cash flow is $50M — far weaker than profit suggests.

Common mistakes

  • Trusting earnings while cash flow keeps lagging (a classic warning sign).
  • Ignoring capex for capital-heavy businesses like airlines or chipmakers.

Key takeaways

  • Cash flow shows real money.
  • Free cash flow = operating cash − capex.
  • Profits that never turn into cash are a red flag.
