Dividends and yield

Beginner · Finance basics

The idea

A dividend is cash a company pays its shareholders out of profits. The dividend yield tells you how much income you get each year for every dollar invested.

How it works

  • Dividend yield = annual dividend per share ÷ share price.
  • If the price falls and the dividend stays, the yield rises — and vice versa.
  • A very high yield can be a warning: the market may expect the dividend to be cut.
  • Reinvesting dividends makes compounding much stronger.

Worked example

A stock at $50 pays $2 a year: yield 4%. If the price drops to $40, the same $2 is a 5% yield. Over the last century, reinvested dividends have made up a large share of total US stock returns.

Common mistakes

  • Chasing the highest yield without asking whether the company can keep paying it.
  • Ignoring taxes on dividends in taxable accounts.

Key takeaways

  • Yield = dividend ÷ price.
  • High yields can signal risk.
  • Reinvested dividends power long-term returns.
