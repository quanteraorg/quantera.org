Inflation and your savings

Beginner · Macro

The idea

Inflation is the general rise in prices over time. It means each dollar buys less, so money kept in cash slowly loses purchasing power.

How it works

  • Real return = investment return − inflation (roughly).
  • At 3% inflation, prices double in about 24 years (Rule of 72).
  • Central banks usually target about 2% inflation and raise rates when it runs higher.

Worked example

$10,000 in cash for 20 years at 3% inflation buys what about $5,500 buys today. A savings account paying 2% during 4% inflation loses about 2% of purchasing power a year.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking cash is "safe" over long periods — it is safe from price swings, not from inflation.
  • Ignoring inflation when planning goals decades away.

Key takeaways

  • Inflation shrinks what money can buy.
  • Look at real (after-inflation) returns.
  • Long-term savings need to outgrow inflation.
