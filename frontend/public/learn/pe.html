The P/E ratio

Beginner · Investing

The idea

The price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio shows how many dollars investors pay for each dollar of a company's yearly profit. It is the most common quick measure of how "expensive" a stock is.

How it works

  • P/E = share price ÷ earnings per share (or market value ÷ total profit).
  • A high P/E means investors expect fast growth; a low P/E can mean low expectations — or a bargain.
  • Compare P/Es within the same industry and against the company's own history.

Worked example

A stock at $60 earning $3 per share has a P/E of 20. A fast-growing software company might trade at 40, a mature bank at 10. Neither is automatically right or wrong.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing P/Es across very different industries.
  • Ignoring that earnings can be temporarily high or low (e.g., in a recession).
  • Forgetting companies with losses have no meaningful P/E.

Key takeaways

  • P/E = price per dollar of profit.
  • High P/E = high growth expectations.
  • Compare like with like.
