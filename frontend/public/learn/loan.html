Loan payments

Beginner · Finance basics

The idea

Loan payments repay interest and part of the original amount (principal) each month. How much you pay overall depends on the rate and how long the loan lasts.

How it works

  • Early payments are mostly interest; later payments are mostly principal (this schedule is called amortisation).
  • A longer term lowers the monthly payment but raises total interest.
  • A small rate difference changes total cost a lot on big, long loans.

Worked example

A $300,000 mortgage over 30 years: at 4% the payment is about $1,432/month and total interest about $216,000; at 7% it is about $1,996/month and total interest about $419,000.

Common mistakes

  • Focusing only on the monthly payment and not the total cost.
  • Not paying extra principal when possible — even small extra payments cut years off a loan.

Key takeaways

  • Payments cover interest first, then principal.
  • Longer term = smaller payment, more interest overall.
  • Rate matters enormously on long loans.
