Diversification

Beginner · Investing

The idea

Diversification means spreading your money across many investments that do not all move together, so one bad outcome cannot sink your whole portfolio.

How it works

  • Each stock has its own risks (a failed product, a lawsuit). Owning many stocks averages those company-specific risks away.
  • What cannot be diversified away is market risk — the whole market falling together.
  • The key is correlation: how closely two investments move together. Adding assets with low correlation reduces risk most.

Worked example

One stock can easily fall 50% in a year. An index fund of 500 stocks fell about 19% in 2022, a bad year — painful, but nothing like the worst single stocks, some of which fell over 70%.

Common mistakes

  • Owning many stocks in one industry ("20 tech stocks") and thinking you are diversified.
  • Over-diversifying into dozens of funds that all hold the same things.
  • Expecting diversification to prevent losses in a broad crash — it reduces risk, it does not remove it.

Key takeaways

  • Spread money across companies, sectors, countries and asset types.
  • Low correlation is what makes diversification work.
  • It removes company risk, not market risk.
