Market capitalization

Beginner · Investing

The idea

Market capitalization (market cap) is the total value the stock market puts on a company: its share price multiplied by the number of shares in existence. It is the usual way to say how big a public company is.

How it works

  • Market cap = share price × shares outstanding.
  • A high share price doesn’t mean a big company: it depends on how many shares exist. Companies can split shares to make each one cheaper without changing the total.
  • Rough size bands: large cap (about $10 billion and up), mid cap ($2–10 billion), small cap (under $2 billion). Smaller companies tend to swing more in price.
  • Enterprise value adds debt and subtracts cash: what it would cost to buy the whole business, including what it owes.

Worked example

A $10 stock with 2 billion shares has a market cap of $20 billion. A $500 stock with 10 million shares: $5 billion. If the first company has $4 billion of debt and $1 billion of cash, its enterprise value is $20B + $4B − $1B = $23 billion.

Common mistakes

  • Judging a company’s size by its share price.
  • Thinking a stock split makes a company more valuable. A 2-for-1 split doubles the shares and halves the price; market cap stays the same.
  • Forgetting that market cap changes every time the share price does.

Key takeaways

  • Market cap = price × shares.
  • Share price alone says nothing about size.
  • Enterprise value adds debt and subtracts cash.
