Position sizing

Intermediate · Trading

The idea

Position sizing decides how much money to put into each trade or investment so that no single loss can do serious damage.

How it works

  • A common rule: risk only 1–2% of your account on any one trade.
  • Shares = (account × risk %) ÷ (entry price − stop price).
  • Wider stops mean smaller positions; tighter stops allow larger ones, for the same risk.

Worked example

Account $20,000, risk 1% = $200. Buy at $50 with a stop at $46 (risk $4 per share): 200 ÷ 4 = 50 shares, a $2,500 position. If the stop is hit you lose about $200.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing size by gut feeling or by how confident you feel.
  • Moving stops further away after entering to "give it room", which quietly increases risk.

Key takeaways

  • Decide the risk first, then the size.
  • 1–2% risk per trade keeps losses survivable.
  • Size depends on the distance to your stop.
