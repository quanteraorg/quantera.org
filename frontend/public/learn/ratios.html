Financial ratios

Intermediate · Accounting

The idea

Financial ratios turn a company's numbers into quick comparisons: how profitable it is, how much debt it carries, and how easily it can pay its bills.

How it works

  • Profitability: net margin (profit ÷ revenue), return on equity (profit ÷ shareholders' equity).
  • Debt: debt-to-equity — how much borrowing compared with owners' money.
  • Liquidity: current ratio (current assets ÷ current liabilities); above 1 means short-term assets cover short-term bills.
  • Valuation: P/E, price-to-sales, price-to-book.

Worked example

Company A: net margin 25%, debt-to-equity 0.3, current ratio 2. Company B: net margin 3%, debt-to-equity 2.5, current ratio 0.8. A is more profitable, less indebted and more liquid.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing ratios across industries with very different norms (banks always carry high leverage).
  • Looking at one ratio in isolation.

Key takeaways

  • Ratios compare companies quickly.
  • Group them: profitability, debt, liquidity, valuation.
  • Compare within an industry and over time.
