Build a DCF

Advanced · Equity research

The idea

A discounted cash flow (DCF) model values a business as the sum of all the cash it will produce in the future, translated into today's money.

How it works

  • Step 1: forecast free cash flow for several years.
  • Step 2: estimate a terminal value for all the years after that (often most of the total).
  • Step 3: discount each future amount back to today: value today = cash ÷ (1 + discount rate)years.
  • The discount rate reflects risk and the return investors require.

Worked example

A company makes $100M of free cash flow growing 5% a year. With a 9% discount rate and 2.5% long-term growth, the value is roughly $1.6B. Change the discount rate to 10% and it drops to about $1.4B — a 1-point change moves the value ~13%.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the output as precise — it is only as good as the assumptions.
  • Using a long-term growth rate above the economy's growth (it would eventually be bigger than the world).
  • Forgetting that the terminal value usually dominates the answer.

Key takeaways

  • Value = future cash in today's money.
  • Higher discount rate → lower value.
  • Test a range of assumptions, not one number.
