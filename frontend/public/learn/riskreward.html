Risk and reward

Beginner · Trading

The idea

Reward-to-risk compares what you could gain with what you could lose. You work it out before you buy, using two prices: a target where you would take profit, and a stop where you would admit you were wrong.

How it works

  • Risk per share = entry − stop. Reward per share = target − entry.
  • Ratio = reward ÷ risk. At 3:1 you stand to make $3 for each $1 risked.
  • Break-even win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + ratio). At 3:1 that is 25%.

Worked example

Entry $100, stop $95, target $115. You risk $5 to make $15: a 3:1 ratio. Risking $200 on the trade means buying $200 ÷ $5 = 40 shares.

Common mistakes

  • Moving the stop further away once the price nears it, which quietly raises your risk.
  • Setting an unrealistic target just to make the ratio look good.

Key takeaways

  • Plan the exit before the entry.
  • The stop sets your position size.
  • A good ratio doesn’t make a trade likely to work.
