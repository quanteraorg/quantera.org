Volume and VWAP

Beginner · Technical analysis

The idea

Volume is the number of shares traded in a period. It tells you how much participation sits behind a price move. VWAP, the Volume-Weighted Average Price, is the average price paid per share, counting busier moments more.

How it works

  • A move on unusually high volume involved more traders than a move on thin volume.
  • VWAP = total dollars traded ÷ total shares traded. It usually restarts each trading day.
  • Trading above VWAP means the current price is higher than the average paid so far.

Worked example

100 shares trade at $10 and 300 shares trade at $12. Total value is $4,600 across 400 shares, so VWAP is $11.50, closer to $12 because more shares traded there. The plain average would be $11.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming high volume tells you the direction. It only shows activity.
  • Comparing raw volume between companies with very different share counts.

Key takeaways

  • Volume shows participation.
  • VWAP is the volume-weighted average price.
  • Compare volume with the stock’s own normal level.
