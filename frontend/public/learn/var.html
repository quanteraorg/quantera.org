Value at Risk

Advanced · Quant

The idea

Value at Risk (VaR) estimates how much a portfolio could lose over a period at a given confidence level, e.g., "a 95% one-day VaR of $10,000".

How it works

  • 95% one-day VaR of $10,000 means on 95% of days losses should be smaller than $10,000.
  • It says nothing about how bad the other 5% of days can be.
  • Expected shortfall (CVaR) averages the losses beyond the VaR point.

Worked example

A $1M portfolio with 1% daily volatility: a 95% one-day VaR is about 1.65 × 1% × $1M ≈ $16,500. On roughly 1 day in 20, losses should exceed that.

Common mistakes

  • Reading VaR as the maximum loss.
  • Assuming normal distributions — real markets have fatter tails.

Key takeaways

  • VaR = a loss threshold at a confidence level.
  • It ignores the size of the worst losses.
  • Pair it with expected shortfall and stress tests.
