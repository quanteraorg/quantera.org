Black-Scholes and the Greeks

Advanced · Derivatives

The idea

The Black-Scholes model prices options from five inputs. The "Greeks" measure how an option's price reacts to each input.

How it works

  • Inputs: stock price, strike, time to expiry, volatility, interest rate.
  • Delta: price change per $1 move in the stock (0 to 1 for calls).
  • Gamma: how fast delta changes.
  • Theta: value lost per day from time decay.
  • Vega: change per 1-point change in volatility.

Worked example

A call with delta 0.6 rises about $0.60 when the stock rises $1. With theta −0.05, it loses about $5 per contract per day if nothing else changes.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming the Greeks stay constant — they change as price and time change.
  • Treating model value as "true value"; real volatility is unknown.

Key takeaways

  • Five inputs drive option prices.
  • Delta, gamma, theta and vega describe sensitivities.
  • Models are approximations.
