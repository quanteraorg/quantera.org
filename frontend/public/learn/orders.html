Market and limit orders

Beginner · Markets

The idea

When you buy or sell a stock you choose an order type. The two basics: a market order (fill now at the best available price) and a limit order (fill only at your price or better).

How it works

  • Market order: executes almost instantly, but the exact price is not guaranteed — in fast or thin markets it can be worse than the last quote.
  • Limit order: you set the maximum you will pay (or minimum you will accept). You control the price but may not get filled.
  • Stop order: becomes a market order once a trigger price is hit, often used to limit losses.
  • The spread is the gap between the best bid (buy) and ask (sell) prices.

Worked example

A stock shows bid $49.98 / ask $50.02. A market buy fills around $50.02. A limit buy at $49.90 waits until a seller accepts $49.90 — which may never happen.

Common mistakes

  • Using market orders on illiquid stocks or at the market open, when spreads are wide.
  • Setting limits far from the price and forgetting about them.

Key takeaways

  • Market = speed, limit = price control.
  • Watch the bid–ask spread.
  • Use limits on thinly traded stocks.
