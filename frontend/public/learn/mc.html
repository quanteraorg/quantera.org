Risk and return paths

Advanced · Quant

The idea

Investing has a range of possible outcomes, not one number. A Monte Carlo simulation runs thousands of random "possible futures" to show how wide that range really is.

How it works

  • Each simulated year draws a random return based on the expected return and volatility.
  • Repeating this many times gives a fan of paths: the middle shows the typical outcome; the edges show lucky and unlucky futures.
  • Higher volatility widens the fan, even with the same average return.

Worked example

$10,000 at an average 7% with 15% volatility over 20 years: the median ends near $38,000, but the unlucky 10% of paths finish under about $17,000 and the lucky 10% above $80,000.

Common mistakes

  • Planning on the average outcome only — you could easily land in the bottom part of the range.
  • Thinking the simulation predicts the future. It only shows possibilities given your assumptions.

Key takeaways

  • Think in ranges, not single numbers.
  • Volatility widens the range of outcomes.
  • Plan so that a bad-luck outcome is still acceptable.
