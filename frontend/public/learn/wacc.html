WACC: cost of capital

Advanced · Equity research

The idea

The weighted average cost of capital (WACC) is the average return a company must pay its lenders and shareholders. It is the usual discount rate in a DCF.

How it works

  • WACC = (E/V × cost of equity) + (D/V × cost of debt × (1 − tax rate)).
  • E = market value of equity, D = debt, V = E + D.
  • Debt is cheaper (interest is tax-deductible) but more debt raises risk for everyone.
  • Projects earning above the WACC create value; below it they destroy value.

Worked example

Equity 70% at 10%, debt 30% at 5% with 25% tax: WACC = 0.7×10% + 0.3×5%×0.75 = 7% + 1.125% = 8.1%.

Common mistakes

  • Using book values instead of market values for weights.
  • Forgetting the tax shield on debt.

Key takeaways

  • WACC blends the cost of equity and debt.
  • It is the hurdle rate for investments.
  • Higher WACC means lower valuations.
