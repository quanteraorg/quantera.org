Recovering from a loss

Beginner · Investing

The idea

A drawdown is a fall from a previous peak. Losses and gains are not symmetrical: after a loss you need a larger percentage gain just to get back to where you were.

How it works

  • Gain needed to recover = loss ÷ (1 − loss).
  • −10% needs +11%; −25% needs +33%; −50% needs +100%; −80% needs +400%.
  • This is why avoiding big losses matters so much for long-term results.

Worked example

The Nasdaq fell about 78% from 2000 to 2002. It needed a gain of about 350% to recover and did not regain its 2000 peak until 2015.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking a 50% loss followed by a 50% gain gets you back to even (it leaves you 25% down).
  • Taking concentrated bets where one large loss could take years to recover.

Key takeaways

  • Bigger losses need much bigger gains to recover.
  • −50% needs +100%.
  • Limiting large losses is central to long-term investing.
