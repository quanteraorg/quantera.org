Compound interest

Beginner · Finance basics

The idea

Compound interest means you earn returns not only on the money you put in, but also on the returns you already earned. Your gains start earning their own gains, so growth speeds up the longer you wait.

How it works

  • Each year your balance grows by the rate of return. Next year the same percentage applies to a bigger balance, so the dollar gain is larger every year.
  • Formula: Future value = Start × (1 + r)years, plus whatever you add along the way.
  • Three inputs matter: how much you save, the rate you earn, and — most of all — how long you leave it.

Worked example

$10,000 at 7% a year: after 10 years it is about $19,700; after 20 years about $38,700; after 30 years about $76,100. The last 10 years add more than the first 20 combined, because the base is so much bigger.

Common mistakes

  • Waiting to start "until I have more money" — lost years cost more than small amounts.
  • Ignoring fees: 1% a year in fees compounds against you just as returns compound for you.
  • Assuming the rate is guaranteed. Stock returns vary a lot from year to year.

Key takeaways

  • Gains earn gains, so growth accelerates over time.
  • Time is the most powerful input.
  • Small, regular contributions add up to large sums.
