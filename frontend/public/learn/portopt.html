Portfolio optimization

Advanced · Quant

The idea

Portfolio optimization chooses how much to hold of each investment to get the best trade-off between expected return and risk (volatility). The key insight of modern portfolio theory is that a portfolio’s risk depends not just on each holding’s risk but on how they move together.

How it works

  • For two assets with weights w and 1 − w: expected return = w·rA + (1 − w)·rB.
  • Volatility = √(w²σA² + (1 − w)²σB² + 2w(1 − w)ρσAσB), where ρ is the correlation between them.
  • If ρ is below 1, the mix is less volatile than the weighted average of the two volatilities. That is diversification.
  • The set of best mixes is the efficient frontier. Real inputs are estimates, so optimized weights can swing a lot when the estimates change.

Worked example

Stock A: 10% expected return, 20% volatility. Stock B: 6% return, 10% volatility. Correlation 0.2. A 50/50 mix has an expected return of 8% and volatility √(0.25·0.04 + 0.25·0.01 + 2·0.25·0.2·0.2·0.1) = √0.0145 ≈ 12.0%, below the 15% weighted average.

Common mistakes

  • Treating estimated returns and correlations as known. Small changes in inputs can change the “optimal” weights a lot.
  • Assuming correlations stay fixed: in market crashes they often rise toward 1.
  • Optimizing return alone and ignoring risk.

Key takeaways

  • Risk depends on correlation, not just each holding.
  • Low correlation gives more diversification.
  • Optimal weights are only as good as the estimates behind them.
