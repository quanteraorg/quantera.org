Fees: small numbers, big impact

Beginner · Investing

The idea

Fees look tiny — 0.5% or 1% a year — but they are charged every year on your whole balance, so they compound against you and can take a huge share of your final wealth.

How it works

  • The expense ratio of a fund is taken from your balance every year whether the fund gains or not.
  • Over decades, a 1% fee can consume around a quarter of your ending balance.
  • Low-cost index funds often charge 0.03%–0.10%.

Worked example

$100,000 for 30 years at 7%: with a 0.05% fee it grows to about $750,000; with a 1% fee about $574,000. That 1% cost you roughly $176,000.

Common mistakes

  • Judging a fee by its size in one year instead of over decades.
  • Assuming higher fees buy better performance — most high-fee active funds trail cheap index funds over time.

Key takeaways

  • Fees compound against you.
  • 1% a year is a big deal over 30 years.
  • Prefer low-cost funds unless there is a clear reason.
