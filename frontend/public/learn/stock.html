What is a stock?

Beginner · Finance basics

The idea

A stock (or share) is a small piece of ownership in a company. If the company grows and earns more, your piece usually becomes worth more; some companies also pay part of their profit to owners as dividends.

How it works

  • Companies sell shares to raise money; after that, investors trade them with each other on an exchange.
  • The price moves with supply and demand, which reflect what investors think future profits will be.
  • You make money two ways: the price rising (capital gain) and dividends.
  • Your maximum loss is what you paid; the possible gain has no ceiling.

Worked example

If a company has 1 billion shares and you own 100, you own one ten-millionth of it. If the company's value rises from $50B to $60B, each share goes from $50 to $60 and your 100 shares from $5,000 to $6,000.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking a low share price means "cheap" — a $5 stock can be more expensive than a $500 one relative to its profits.
  • Confusing the company with the stock: a great company can be a poor investment if the price already assumes perfection.

Key takeaways

  • A share is part-ownership of a business.
  • Prices reflect expectations of future profits.
  • Returns come from price gains and dividends.
