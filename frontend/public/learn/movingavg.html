Moving averages

Beginner · Technical analysis

The idea

A moving average is the average closing price over a set number of days, recalculated every day. Plotted as a line, it smooths out the noise so you can see which way the price has been heading.

How it works

  • Simple (SMA): every day counts equally. Exponential (EMA): recent days count more, so it reacts faster.
  • Short averages (9 or 20 days) follow the price closely. Long ones (50 or 200 days) show the bigger trend.
  • When a short average crosses above a long one, some traders see improving momentum. It can also be a false signal.

Worked example

Closing prices over 5 days of $10, $11, $12, $11 and $13 add up to $57, so the 5-day average is $57 ÷ 5 = $11.40. Tomorrow the oldest day drops out and the newest comes in.

Common mistakes

  • Expecting a moving average to predict the future. It is built from past prices, so it always lags.
  • Trading every crossover in a choppy, sideways market, where they often fail.

Key takeaways

  • It shows the trend, not the future.
  • Longer means smoother but slower.
  • Use it with other evidence.
