Reading an income statement

Beginner · Accounting

The idea

An income statement shows how much a company sold, what it spent, and what was left as profit over a period (a quarter or a year).

How it works

  • Revenue (sales) at the top.
  • Minus cost of goods sold = gross profit.
  • Minus operating expenses (salaries, marketing, R&D) = operating income.
  • Minus interest and taxes = net income (the "bottom line"). Divided by shares = earnings per share.
  • Margins (profit ÷ revenue) show how much of each sales dollar is kept.

Worked example

Revenue $1,000M, cost of goods $600M → gross profit $400M (40% margin). Operating costs $250M → operating income $150M (15%). Interest and tax $50M → net income $100M (10% net margin).

Common mistakes

  • Looking only at revenue growth while margins shrink.
  • Ignoring one-off items that make a single quarter look unusually good or bad.

Key takeaways

  • Revenue → gross profit → operating income → net income.
  • Margins show efficiency.
  • Read trends over several periods.
