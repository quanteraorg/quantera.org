Bollinger Bands

Intermediate · Technical analysis

The idea

Bollinger Bands draw a line above and below a moving average, set by how much the price has been varying. They give a quick picture of volatility: how wide the recent swings have been.

How it works

  • The middle line is usually a 20-day average.
  • The bands are normally 2 standard deviations above and below it.
  • Bands widen when swings grow and narrow when the price calms down.

Worked example

If the 20-day average is $100 and the standard deviation is $3, the bands sit at $106 and $94 ($100 ± 2 × $3).

Common mistakes

  • Assuming a touch of the upper band means the stock must fall. In strong trends, prices can ride a band for weeks.
  • Reading a narrow “squeeze” as a sign of direction. It says nothing about which way a move might go.

Key takeaways

  • Bands measure volatility.
  • A touch is not a signal by itself.
  • A squeeze says a bigger move may come, not its direction.
