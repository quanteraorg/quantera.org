Leverage: gains and losses

Intermediate · Trading

The idea

Leverage means investing with borrowed money. It magnifies both gains and losses relative to your own money.

How it works

  • With 2× leverage, a 10% move in the investment becomes about a 20% move in your equity.
  • Borrowing costs interest, which eats into returns.
  • If losses shrink your equity too far, the broker issues a margin call and may sell your holdings at the worst time.

Worked example

You put in $10,000 and borrow $10,000 to buy $20,000 of stock. A 20% rise makes $4,000 (+40% on your money). A 20% fall loses $4,000 (−40%). A 50% fall wipes you out completely.

Common mistakes

  • Focusing on magnified gains and ignoring magnified losses.
  • Holding leveraged ETFs long-term without understanding daily resets, which erode value in choppy markets.

Key takeaways

  • Leverage multiplies gains and losses.
  • Borrowing has a cost.
  • Margin calls can force selling at the bottom.
