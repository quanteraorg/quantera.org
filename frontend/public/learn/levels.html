Support, resistance and trendlines

Beginner · Technical analysis

The idea

Support is a price area where a falling stock has repeatedly stopped. Resistance is an area where a rising stock has repeatedly stalled. A trendline connects rising lows or falling highs to show the direction of travel.

How it works

  • Levels are zones more than exact prices. Allow a little room.
  • An uptrend line connects at least two rising lows. A downtrend line connects falling highs.
  • An old resistance, once broken, sometimes becomes support, and the other way around.

Worked example

A stock rises to about $150 in March, June and August, and turns down each time. $150 is resistance. On Quantera’s chart, pick the Horizontal line tool and click at $150 to mark it. Your drawing saves with the chart.

Common mistakes

  • Forcing lines onto a chart to fit what you already want to believe.
  • Assuming a level must hold. Levels show where the price reacted before, not where it will next time.

Key takeaways

  • Support: where falls have paused.
  • Resistance: where rises have paused.
  • Treat levels as zones, and have a plan if they break.
