How stock markets work

Beginner · Markets

The idea

A stock market is where shares in companies are bought and sold. A company first sells shares to raise money (for example in an IPO); after that, investors trade those shares with each other on exchanges such as the NYSE and Nasdaq. The company doesn’t set the price: trades between buyers and sellers do.

How it works

  • The bid is the highest price a buyer is offering right now; the ask is the lowest price a seller will accept. The gap between them is the spread.
  • A trade happens when a buyer and a seller agree. The last trade price is what you see quoted as “the price”.
  • Market makers stand ready to buy and sell, which keeps trading smooth; the spread is part of how they are paid.
  • An index such as the S&P 500 tracks a basket of stocks, so you can see how the market as a whole moved.

Worked example

If the bid is $99.90 and the ask is $100.10, the spread is $0.20, or 0.2% of the price. Buying 100 shares at the ask and selling straight back at the bid would lose $20 before any fees.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking the company sets or receives money from each trade. After the IPO, trades are between investors.
  • Ignoring the spread on thinly traded stocks, where it can be large.
  • Expecting to trade at the last price shown: you buy at the ask and sell at the bid.

Key takeaways

  • Prices come from trades between buyers and sellers.
  • You buy at the ask and sell at the bid.
  • An index shows how a whole market moved.
