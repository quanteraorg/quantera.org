Options: calls and puts

Intermediate · Derivatives

The idea

Options are contracts on a stock. A call profits if the price rises above the strike; a put profits if it falls below. Buyers pay a premium; their loss is limited to that premium.

How it works

  • Call: the right to buy at the strike price before expiry.
  • Put: the right to sell at the strike price before expiry.
  • Each US stock option usually covers 100 shares.
  • At expiry, a call is worth (stock − strike) if positive; a put (strike − stock) if positive.

Worked example

Stock at $100. You buy a $105 call for $2 (×100 = $200). At expiry the stock is $112: the call is worth $7 → $700, profit $500. If the stock is $104, the call expires worthless and you lose $200.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting the premium when calculating break-even (strike + premium for a call).
  • Selling options without understanding the possibly large losses.

Key takeaways

  • Calls bet on rises, puts on falls (or protect against them).
  • Buyers risk only the premium.
  • Break-even = strike ± premium.
