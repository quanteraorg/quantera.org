PEG: price versus growth

Intermediate · Investing

The idea

The PEG ratio adjusts the P/E ratio for growth, so you can compare a fast grower and a slow grower more fairly.

How it works

  • PEG = P/E ÷ expected annual earnings growth (%).
  • Around 1 is often called fairly priced; below 1 may be cheap for its growth; above 2 may be expensive.
  • It depends heavily on the growth forecast, which can be wrong.

Worked example

Stock A: P/E 30, growth 30% → PEG 1.0. Stock B: P/E 15, growth 5% → PEG 3.0. Despite the higher P/E, A looks cheaper relative to its growth.

Common mistakes

  • Trusting optimistic growth forecasts.
  • Using PEG for companies with negative or very unstable earnings.

Key takeaways

  • PEG = P/E ÷ growth.
  • It compares value across growth rates.
  • Only as good as the growth estimate.
