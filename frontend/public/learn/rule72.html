The Rule of 72

Beginner · Finance basics

The idea

The Rule of 72 is a quick mental trick: divide 72 by an annual growth rate to estimate how many years it takes for money to double.

How it works

  • Years to double ≈ 72 ÷ rate (%).
  • It works for anything that compounds: investments, inflation, debt.
  • It is most accurate for rates between about 4% and 12%.

Worked example

At 8% a year, money doubles in about 72 ÷ 8 = 9 years. At 3% inflation, prices double in about 24 years — so $100 of groceries today costs about $200 in 24 years.

Common mistakes

  • Using it for very high rates (at 50% it is noticeably off).
  • Forgetting it also works against you: credit-card debt at 24% doubles in about 3 years.

Key takeaways

  • 72 ÷ rate = years to double.
  • Use it for investments, inflation and debt.
  • A few extra percent shortens doubling time a lot.
