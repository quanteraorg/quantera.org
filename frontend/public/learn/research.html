How to research a company

Beginner · Equity research

The idea

Researching a company means answering a few plain questions and writing down your reasoning. Is the business growing? Does it make money? Can it pay its bills? What are you paying for it? And what could go wrong?

How it works

  • Start with the scorecard on any stock page. Each card shows the checks behind it.
  • Read the financials: look for the direction of sales, profit and free cash flow over several years.
  • Read the news and filings to find out why the numbers changed.
  • Write it down in your notes: your reasons, your risks, and what would change your mind.

Worked example

A company’s sales grow 25% a year with 20% margins, but its P/E is 80. The business scores well while the price is high, so a useful note might be: “Great business. The risk is that it’s already priced for perfection.”

Common mistakes

  • Stopping at the first number that agrees with you.
  • Comparing a bank’s debt or a software company’s margins with those of a retailer. Compare within an industry.
  • Treating any score as a buy or sell signal.

Key takeaways

  • Business quality and price are separate questions.
  • Write down what would prove you wrong.
  • Compare companies with their own industry.
