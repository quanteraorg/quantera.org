The Kelly criterion

Advanced · Quant

The idea

The Kelly criterion calculates the bet size that maximises long-term growth when you know your odds. In practice most people use a fraction of it because estimates are uncertain.

How it works

  • For a simple bet: Kelly % = W − (1 − W) ÷ R, where W is win probability and R is win/loss ratio.
  • Betting more than Kelly increases risk and actually lowers long-run growth.
  • "Half Kelly" keeps most of the growth with much smaller swings.

Worked example

You win 55% of the time and wins equal losses (R = 1): Kelly = 0.55 − 0.45 = 10% of your bankroll per bet. Half Kelly would be 5%.

Common mistakes

  • Plugging in overconfident win rates — errors push you to oversize.
  • Using full Kelly and enduring huge drawdowns.

Key takeaways

  • Kelly sizes bets to maximise growth.
  • Over-betting destroys growth.
  • Use a fraction because your edge is uncertain.
