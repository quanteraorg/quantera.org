Nvidia's Q2 FY2027 results: an 8.7% jump, then half of it given back

Revenue doubled and guidance rose by about $12 billion. The stock beat the market by 8 points on the first day, then gave back about half the next. What the numbers show, and what they can't.

Earnings analysis · Published 10 November 2026 · 6 min read · By Quantera Research · NVDA

Research question

What did Nvidia report for its most recently reported quarter, and how did its shares move on the first trading day after the release and over the following four sessions, compared with the S&P 500?

Summary

Nvidia's revenue for the quarter ended 26 July 2026 was $96.2 billion, up 106% from a year earlier and 18% from the previous quarter. Data Center revenue was $89.0 billion (+117% year on year).

The results came after the market closed on 26 August, so the first trading reaction was on 27 August. Nvidia's shares closed up 8.74% that day while the S&P 500 rose 0.72%: an abnormal move of about +8.0 percentage points.

The next day the shares fell 4.57%, giving back about half of the first day's gain. Over the five sessions from 27 August to 2 September they rose 7.04% against −0.12% for the S&P 500.

Key findings

  • Revenue $96.2B: +106% year on year, +18% on the previous quarter.
  • Gross margin 75.0%; guidance for the next quarter: $108.0B revenue (±2%) at a 74.0% gross margin, excluding any China Data Center compute revenue.
  • GAAP earnings per share ($2.46) were higher than non-GAAP ($2.22) because of about $7.8B of net gains on equity investments, which non-GAAP figures leave out.
  • Day 0 (27 Aug): NVDA +8.74% vs S&P 500 +0.72%, abnormal +8.02 points.
  • Day +1 (28 Aug): NVDA −4.57%, about half of the day-0 gain given back.
  • Days 0 to +4: NVDA +7.04% vs S&P 500 −0.12%, abnormal +7.15 points.

InterpretationInterpretation, not fact

The first reaction was strongly positive, which fits revenue growth of more than 100% and an outlook roughly $12 billion above the quarter just reported.

The partial reversal on the next day coincided with coverage of the slightly lower gross-margin outlook (74.0% vs 75.0%), the outlook's exclusion of China Data Center revenue, supply limits and a reported pause of a revenue-sharing program for smaller AI cloud providers [5]. These events happened at the same time as the moves; that does not prove they caused them.

Without a verified consensus forecast, we cannot say by how much the results beat or missed what investors expected, and expectations, not results alone, usually drive the size of an earnings reaction.

Method

  1. The latest reported quarter was identified from Nvidia's SEC filings. Results for the following quarter were scheduled for 17 November 2026 [2].
  2. Day 0 is the first session after the release (after-close news counts from the next session).
  3. Daily return = close ÷ previous close − 1. The five-session return = close on 2 Sep ÷ close on 26 Aug − 1.
  4. Abnormal return = Nvidia's return − the S&P 500's return (a simple market-adjusted model with no beta adjustment).
  5. All arithmetic was re-run independently before publication.

Limitations

  • One event only. It shows what happened this time, not a pattern.
  • Prices come from a data aggregator (Yahoo Finance), not the exchange; S&P 500 closes rely mainly on that one source.
  • "After the close" is inferred from the 5 p.m. call time.
  • The abnormal return doesn't adjust for Nvidia's higher-than-average sensitivity to the market (beta). Quantera's Insights tool does: try the comparison below.
  • The five-session figure depends on the window chosen, because day 0 was partly reversed on day +1.
  • Analysts' consensus estimates were not found.
