OpenAI vs Anthropic: how the AI race flipped, in their own numbers

In March 2025, Anthropic was valued at about a fifth of OpenAI. By May 2026 it was valued higher. Now Anthropic is heading to the stock market. What the filings show, and why "who makes more money" depends on how you count.

Comparative analysis · Published November 16, 2026 · Updated November 21, 2026 · {a.get("readMinutes", 5)} min read · By Quantera Research

Update, November 21, 2026

Since this study was published on November 16, 2026, Anthropic has priced its IPO at $83 a share, above the range discussed below, and its shares began trading on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT on November 19, closing their first day at $97.17. Research question

How do OpenAI and Anthropic compare on valuation, revenue and what outside investors say their shares are worth, using primary filings wherever they exist?

Summary

Anthropic's private valuation rose from $61.5 billion in March 2025 to $965 billion in May 2026, briefly above OpenAI's latest round of $852 billion, before OpenAI moved back ahead at about $1.25 trillion in November 2026 [10][3][7].

Anthropic's IPO filing shows revenue of $33.2 billion in the first nine months of 2026, up from $2.5 billion a year earlier, and its first quarterly operating profit, in Q2 2026 [1]. OpenAI is still private: its figures come from what it tells investors, as reported by the press, and are not audited [4][5][6].

The two companies count revenue differently, so a simple comparison misleads. A Fidelity fund valued Anthropic's shares 32% above its last round in August; Anthropic's IPO price range, set on 10 November, values it close to that last round instead [2][1].

Key findings

  • The valuation gap closed in 14 months: Anthropic went from about one fifth of OpenAI's valuation ($61.5B vs $300B, March 2025) to above it ($965B in May 2026 vs OpenAI's $852B) [10][3][7].
  • Anthropic's revenue grew 13× in a year: $33.2B in January–September 2026 vs $2.5B a year earlier; quarterly revenue rose from $376M (Q1 2025) to $17.0B (Q3 2026) [1].
  • Anthropic made its first operating profit in Q2 2026 ($974M, after stock-based compensation), then an operating loss of $567M in Q3 as computing costs nearly doubled [1].
  • "Who earns more" depends on accounting: Anthropic records the full price of sales made through cloud partners; OpenAI records only its share. OpenAI's ~$50B annualized revenue becomes ~$70B on Anthropic's method [4][5].
  • On 31 Aug 2026 a Fidelity fund valued Anthropic shares at $777.53, 32% above the $589.01 May round price (implying about $1.27T), while still valuing OpenAI at its March round price [2].
  • The IPO range of $70–$78 a share values Anthropic at $916B–$1.02T: close to the May round, about 24% below Fidelity's August mark [1][2].
  • Anthropic's $104B net loss for January–September 2026 is mostly an accounting effect: $94.3B came from revaluing convertible notes and SAFEs as its value rose; the operating loss was $1.26B [1].

Two companies, one race

OpenAI makes ChatGPT; Anthropic makes Claude. Both build frontier AI models and sell access to consumers and businesses, and both are public benefit corporations. Anthropic's IPO filing names OpenAI and Google as its main competitors [1].

The biggest difference today is disclosure. Anthropic has filed to list on Nasdaq, so it has published audited annual and reviewed quarterly statements [1]. OpenAI is still private: its revenue figures come from what it tells investors, reported by the press, and are not audited. Treat every OpenAI revenue figure here as reported, not verified.

The valuation race

Each private funding round sets a price per share and, from it, a value for the whole company (post-money valuation unless stated). Between March 2025 and May 2026, OpenAI's valuation rose about 2.8× ($300B to $852B) while Anthropic's rose about 15.7× ($61.5B to $965B) [10].

OpenAI's revenue, and why it isn't apples to apples

Reported (unaudited): Q1 2026 revenue $5.7B and Q2 2026 $6.7B, with a Q2 operating loss of $12.3B including stock-based compensation, up from $9.3B in Q1 [6]. September 2026 annualized revenue was about $50B [4][5], and OpenAI has told investors it expects at least $70B annualized by the end of 2026 [8].

The catch: if a customer pays $100 for AI through a cloud provider, Anthropic records the full $100 as revenue and the provider's cut as an expense; OpenAI records only its share. Both methods follow US accounting rules. Grossed up to Anthropic's method for investors, OpenAI's annualized revenue was about $70B rather than $50B [4]. Anthropic's Q3 2026 revenue annualizes to $67.8B (calculated: $16.956B × 4) [1].

On reported numbers Anthropic overtook OpenAI in Q2 2026 ($11.6B vs $6.7B). On a like-for-like basis we can't say for certain who is bigger, because OpenAI hasn't published comparable statements. The honest reading: they are now similar in size, with Anthropic growing faster.

What a fund manager says the shares are worth

Mutual funds that own private shares report what they think they are worth in public SEC filings (Form N-PORT). These are Level 3 valuations: the fund's own estimate, not a market price [2].

Calculated: $777.53 ÷ $589.01 = 1.32, so Fidelity's August mark was 32% above Anthropic's May round. Applied to the $965B round valuation, that implies about $1.27T, above OpenAI's ≈$1.25T November round. This is one fund family's estimate, it assumes the same share count as the May round, and fund marks lag and can be revised.

  2. Shares offered: 825,000,000 (801,351,354 new; 23,648,646 sold by existing holders), plus a private placement at the IPO price of about $10B each from Google and NVIDIA at the midpoint, and AMD [1].
  3. Shares outstanding after the offering: 13,086,672,593. Calculated value: $916B at $70, $968B at $74, $1.02T at $78 [1].
  4. Cash and short-term investments: $131.8B on 30 Sep 2026; $212.7B pro forma after the offering [1].
  5. Rough price-to-sales (calculated): about 14× Q3 2026 revenue annualized at the midpoint ($968B ÷ $67.8B). For OpenAI, about 25× its ≈$50B annualized figure, or about 18× the ≈$70B grossed-up figure. Annualized figures are projections, so treat these as rough.

Why Anthropic shows a $104B loss while turning an operating profit

Investors holding convertible notes and SAFEs are owed shares. When the company's value rises, what it owes them rises too, and that is booked as a loss; these items are expected to largely disappear when they convert into shares at the IPO [1]. Plain-English rule: when a fast-growing private company reports a huge net loss, check how much of it is fair-value changes.

InterpretationInterpretation, not fact

The filings support a narrow conclusion: in 2026 Anthropic grew revenue much faster than OpenAI and closed most of the valuation gap, while both remain far from steady profits once computing costs are counted.

They do not settle which company is bigger, because the two record revenue differently and only Anthropic publishes audited statements. The IPO price, and how ANT trades afterwards, will be the first market price for either company.

Limitations

  • OpenAI's revenue and losses are reported by the press from investor communications; they are unaudited and may be defined differently from Anthropic's.
  • Private valuations come from Forge Global's record of funding rounds; reports differ on whether OpenAI's $1.25T is before or after new money (under 2% apart).
  • Fund marks are one manager's Level 3 estimate on one date; the implied company value assumes the May round's share count.
  • Annualized revenue multiplies one period by four or twelve; it is a projection, not a reported annual figure.
  • Anthropic's IPO had not been priced on the publication date; the range and share counts can change before pricing.
  • Data as of November 16, 2026.
