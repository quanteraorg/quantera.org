About Quantera Research

Quantera Research is a student-led financial research, education and technology initiative. We study how companies and markets react to the news, publish what we find with every source shown, and build free tools so anyone can check the work.

Why we started

Financial news tells you what happened. It rarely explains why the same story moves one company 5% and another 25%, how unusual a move really was, or what the numbers in a company’s filings actually say. The tools that join all of this up are built for professionals and cost thousands a year, and much of what is free is scattered, full of jargon or unsourced.

We wanted one place where the research comes first, every number shows where it came from, and learning is built in rather than bolted on.

Who we are

Quantera was founded by twin brothers Aarav Gupta and Aryan Gupta, who are students. They research, write, design and build Quantera together. It is an independent initiative: we are not a bank, broker, investment adviser or research firm, and we have no outside investors or institutional partners.

How we research

  • Primary sources first. Company filings with the SEC, official releases and exchange data come before news coverage or opinion.
  • No source, no number. If we can’t verify a figure, we say it wasn’t found rather than estimate it.
  • Facts apart from interpretation. What happened is labeled separately from what it might mean. Things that happen at the same time are never presented as cause and effect.
  • Checked before publishing. Every calculation is re-run independently, and every study states its limits.
  • Dated and corrected. Every study shows when it was published. When newer results or events change the picture, we add a dated update note at the top rather than silently rewriting it.

What you’ll find here

  • Research: our published studies of companies, earnings and market events.
  • Insights: how we compare the way different companies react to the same news, measured against the market and each stock’s normal day.
  • Company research: financial statements from SEC filings, valuation, competitors and earnings history for US stocks.
  • Academy: short lessons and exercises, from reading a chart to valuing a company.
  • Practice: paper trading with $100,000 of virtual money, always labeled as practice. Nothing real is ever bought.

Our educational mission

Learning finance should start from real companies and real events, not abstract definitions. Every lesson links to a company you can open, every ratio explains how it is calculated, and the Academy includes exercises where you interpret what actually happened before seeing the answer.

What’s next

Our long-term research question is how companies of different sizes react to comparable events: how large the moves are, how long they last and how they recover. We are building that dataset one study at a time, and we hope to open it to other students who want to research with us.
